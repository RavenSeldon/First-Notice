# Limitations

- **Simulated, not operational:** the registry, routing, recipients and clocks are simulated. The 48-hour notice clock and the 24-hour acknowledgment are proposals, not law. Sender verification uses fixture credentials, not cryptographic authentication.
- **Narrow gap, honestly stated:** Canada already requires departmental incident reporting (C15) and contractual supplier reporting (C19). The proposal adds a uniform, AI-agent-specific early-notification pathway; it does not fill an empty field.
- **Reach of procurement:** it covers participating federal contracts only; not unrelated research, suppliers without such contracts, or unknown operators of open-weight models.
- **Disclosure incentives:** mandatory reporting can discourage disclosure if confidentiality, liability and competitive consequences are badly designed (C10, C13).
- **Source limits:** C07, C09, C20 and C22 rest on the developer's own account. The exact mid-August discovery date is not public; the counterfactual uses 15 August as a stand-in. The government's forensic investigation was not complete in this record.
- **What the prototype shows:** that the protocol behaves as specified on documented, counterfactual and synthetic cases. It does not show that real-world harm would have been prevented, or how it would perform in production.
