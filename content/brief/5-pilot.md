# From Prototype to Public Test

Canada need not begin with a nationwide reporting mandate. A time-limited pilot could apply to selected federal contracts for suppliers of AI systems capable of acting through external tools or networks. Each contract would define covered activity, the discovery threshold that starts the 48-hour reporting clock, a designated departmental recipient and an escalation route through existing cybersecurity coordination. The pilot would supplement, not replace, established reporting obligations [C15, C19].

Success should be measured against existing practice, not by the number of notices filed. Evaluation should examine four outcomes:

- **Timeliness:** Time from qualifying discovery to the first actionable notice.
- **Acknowledgment:** Whether provisional T1 notices receive acknowledgment within 24 hours.
- **Evidence quality:** Completeness of reports, clarity about uncertainty, and preservation of subsequent corrections.
- **Administrative burden:** False-positive rates, verification effort, and staff time required to process notices.

Participating organizations would establish baseline measures and consistent audit procedures before the pilot. Costs would include maintaining a contact registry, staffing and verifying notices, supplier compliance, secure information handling and auditing. Any financial estimate would require a defined scope and transparent costing method.

The First Notice prototype tests the proposed rules against documented, counterfactual and synthetic cases. It demonstrates whether the protocol behaves as specified under those test conditions. It does not establish that real-world harm would have been prevented or that the system would perform equally well in production.

The Australian record revealed two delays: discovering unauthorized activity and communicating that discovery to the affected government. A reporting protocol cannot guarantee earlier detection. What Canada can test is whether a credible warning, once discovered, reaches the right authority sooner—with sufficient information to act and at an acceptable administrative cost [C01, C02, C03].
